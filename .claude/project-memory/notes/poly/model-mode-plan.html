Model mode: a pen-first poly modeler

A new Model page on the shelf turns an object into an editable cage of quads and polygons. You hover a point, an edge or a face, see what will happen, and press and drag with the pen to do it, as in ZBrush's ZModeler. The cage shows a live smooth surface with sharp creases, and one tap turns it into a sculptable object with subdivision levels. Retopology over a sculpt uses the same cage and the same actions.

This plan builds on what I read: ZModeler and poly modeling: what I read, which now also covers Shapr3D, Blender, Plasticity, 3D-Coat, Maya's Quad Draw and Modo's Topology Pen, and says what each adds for a pen. It uses the default answers to the seven questions at the end of that doc, which are listed again at the bottom of this page. Nothing gets built until you approve.

The pen rules

Every feature below had to pass these rules. They come from what makes ZModeler work on an XP-Pen, and from what makes Blender, Maya and Plasticity hard to use with one.

  • One press-drag does the job.Hover the element, press, drag, lift. No action needs a separate selection step or a key held down.
  • The drag sets the amount.The distance sets how far or how wide, and dragging sideways sets a count (loops, segments) where an action has one. The value floats beside the pen.
  • A number is one tap away.Tap the floating value to type it, as in Shapr3D and Plasticity. After you lift, Adjust Last keeps every setting editable, as in Blender.
  • You see it before you do it.Hover shows the element in its colour and a label such as "Extrude · Island". On a pen without hover, tap the element to get a handle on it, as Shapr3D does: an arrow on a face, a double arrow on an edge.
  • Timing never changes meaning.A pause before you move never turns one action into another, which is Shapr3D's most common complaint. Holding still only shows a preview.
  • Variants never need a keyboard.ZModeler's Alt, Shift and Ctrl become the three sectors of the modifier wheel you already have. A thumb holds one while the pen drags; keys and pen buttons are only shortcuts.
  • Tap repeats or adds.After Extrude or Insert loop, a tap on another element repeats it with the same amount, as in ZModeler. After Bevel, Crease or Inset, a tap adds the element to the same action until you tap empty space, as in Shapr3D. A movement tolerance stops stray taps, and you can turn repeating off.
  • A selection suggests what to do.When you select instead of hovering, a short strip of the actions that fit it appears beside the pen, as Plasticity's Suggested Commands do. Repeated drags on the same handle add up to one total instead of starting again at zero.
  • Touch helps.Fingers orbit, pan and zoom as now, hold wheel sectors, and tap shelf tiles. Fingers never need to hit a small edge.

What hover shows

The pen picks whatever is nearest: a point within 10 pixels, else an edge within 8, else the face under it. Each kind has its own colour, as in the newer ZBrush highlights, so you know what the next drag will touch. Where things overlap, a second tap on the same spot picks the next one underneath.

One action, start to finish

  1. HoverThe element lights up and the label says what will happen. On a pen without hover, tap it to get its handle instead.
  2. Press and dragThe amount follows the pen. Dragging the other way gives the opposite where it makes sense, such as extruding in instead of out.
  3. Hold a sector if neededYour thumb holds Alt for the variant, or Snap to lock steps and weld. It can change in the middle of the drag.
  4. LiftThe action commits as one undo step. Its main values stay on the canvas, and its card holds every value it used.
  5. Tap to repeat or addTap another element to repeat the action, or to add it to a bevel or crease. Tap empty space to finish.

Ideas from other apps

Kept, because they work with a pen

  • Blender: Adjust Last, loop cut with a count, dissolve, grid fill, shortest path, select linked, proportional editing, the bevel's quality and profile, the knife as a pen stroke, n-gons while blocking out, bevel miters and clamping, limited dissolve, merge by distance, select similar, loop shaping, mirror clipping and the mesh analysis colours.
  • Shapr3D: handles on the tapped face or edge for pens without hover, the sign of a bevel picking round or flat, values you tap to type with sums and scrubbing, parameter cards that keep values on the canvas, actions that take more taps until you tap empty space, faces that move while the shape stays closed, and Align.
  • Plasticity: the drag handle with a typed value beside it, the selection that readies the likely action, suggested actions for a selection, one handle for round and flat, drags that add up, and reselecting the last inputs.
  • Valence 3D: loops picked automatically from one tap.
  • Maya: Quad Draw's pen strokes on the live surface for retopology, multi-cut's tap to tap cutting, marking menus (our pie).
  • 3D-Coat: strokes that become strips, tubes and filled areas of quads, Spine, the mirror ghost, open edges in red, and hover-and-tap tweak tools.
  • Modo: the Topology Pen's loop switch, welds shown while dragging and made on lift, and never picking hidden surfaces.
  • Nomad: primitives with handles for their settings, and a clear step from a cage to a sculpt.

Dropped, because they need a keyboard or a mouse

  • Blender's G, R and S with typed axes, and box select as the main way to choose things.
  • Selecting first and then running a command from a menu, which every desktop modeler relies on. Hover and target replace it.
  • Maya's option dialogs before an action runs. Adjust Last comes after the action.
  • ZModeler's TransPose actions, which our move gizmo already covers.
  • Modo's action centres and falloff setup panels. The Snap sector and soft falloff cover the common cases.
  • Shapr3D's double tap that selects a whole body, and its tap, hold and drag rules that depend on timing.
  • Maya's and Modo's mouse-button grammar (Tab with a middle drag, Ctrl Shift click) and Plasticity's letter keys inside a command. Their jobs move to the wheel sectors and the pie.

What changes in the code

Today everything is triangles: geom.Mesh stores triangles only, OBJ files are split into triangles on import, and the quads from retopology are kept on the side in sculpt.Levels.faces and drawn as triangle pairs. Subdivision levels use Loop subdivision, which only works on triangles and has no creases. Polygroups are stored per triangle, and the wireframe draws triangle edges. Modeling needs real polygons, so the cage lives in its own structure and sculpting stays on triangles.

  • New package src/polyA half-edge mesh for the cage: polygons of any size, with fast walks around faces, points and loops. Pure geometry, like geom: no rendering, no interface. Edges with more than two faces are split when a cage is made, and the reason is shown. 
    Poly_Mesh :: struct {
	positions:  [dynamic][3]f32,
	vert_out:   [dynamic]u32, // one half-edge leaving each point
	he_next:    [dynamic]u32, // next half-edge around its face
	he_from:    [dynamic]u32, // the point it leaves
	he_face:    [dynamic]u32, // NONE on a border
	face_first: [dynamic]u32,
	face_group: [dynamic]u16,
	crease:     [dynamic]f32, // per edge, edge = he >> 1, twin = he ~ 1
	...
}
  • Actions as pure proceduresEach action takes the cage, a resolved target and its values, and returns what changed. Targets (loops, rings, islands, flat islands, groups, shortest path) are queries in the same package. Results are deterministic, so serial and parallel runs match and tests can compare them.
  • Drawing and pickingThe cage is cut into triangles for drawing and hit testing, with a map from each triangle back to its face. Cage edges are drawn as thin screen-space strips by a small new pipeline, so they show on devices that cannot draw lines (the current wireframe turns off there) and can carry crease, selection and hover colours.
  • Catmull-Clark subdivisionThe smooth surface with semi-sharp creases follows the OpenSubdiv rules, so it matches Blender. During a drag only the faces near the change are recomputed. Results are checked against Blender's Subdivision Surface using the Blender we already use in scripts/export_check.py.
  • Subdivision levels learn Catmull-Clarksculpt.Levels gets a second scheme beside Loop, and Quad_Faces becomes a general face list. Apply to sculpt then builds levels from the cage. This also gives sculpting on quad levels, which ZBrush uses.
  • Objects keep their cageAn object can hold a cage and its live surface settings. The sculptor renumbers points for speed, so a stored map links cage points to mesh points. Polygroups go from cage faces to their triangles.
  • UndoEach action records a snapshot of the cage in the History as one entry. A cage of 100,000 faces is a few megabytes. Adjust Last replaces that entry instead of adding one.
  • Scene file version 26Adds the cage (points, faces, groups, creases, live settings) to objects. Every older file still opens. OBJ and USD export write real quads and polygons; glTF splits them into triangles as it must.
  • InterfaceA Model tool and shelf page, the wheel's modeling labels, the element pie, presets as tiles, handles for pens without hover, the floating value, a parameter card per action built on Adjust Last, and Settings for tap-to-repeat and pick distances. Selection changes are recorded in the History so they can be undone. New scene commands for making a cage and applying it.

Risks

  • Bevel qualityClean bevel corners are hard; Blender spent years on them. The first version handles loops and corners where three or four edges meet, and says when it cannot bevel something rather than making bad geometry.
  • Creases matching other appsSemi-sharp creases have exact rules. Tests compare our surface with Blender's for the same cage.
  • Picking small edgesOn a tablet an edge can be a few pixels wide. Pick distances are settings, and the label always shows what will be hit.
  • Live smoothing while draggingRecomputing a big smooth surface every frame is slow, so only the faces near the change are redone. If that is not enough, the drag shows one level less until you lift.
  • Pens without hover on AndroidHandles cover it, but they take one more tap. That is worth trying on your tablet early, after phase 3.
