Study · round 2 · 2 Nov 2026

What Uniform teaches us

Round 1 listed Uniform features to copy. Bolt asked for something better: learn how Uniform found ideas Nomad doesn't have, find our own, check whether brush modulators matter in real work, and feel free to rewrite, replace or remove features. This round answers those questions, using what artists actually say about Uniform, Procreate, Krita, Photoshop and ZBrush brush packs.

The short answer

  • Uniform's new ideas all come from one move: a small set of tools that work on every kind of data, plus switches that change every tool at once. That is where its features no other app has come from.
  • The same move is why artists bounce off it. The Nomad forum thread at launch is mostly "daunting", "no idea where to start" and one refund. Its App Store rating is 3.4 from 25 reviews.
  • Brush modulators pay off in real work only as presets. Painters keep pressure on size and opacity and rarely touch the rest. In sculpting, the demand is for repeated detail (stitches, chains, scales), and today people buy that as brush packs. Uniform's own examples are rainbows and dashes.
  • So we copy the composition and hide the wiring. Our original ideas are listed below: strokes you can re-tune afterwards, brushes that read the sculpt, a look that follows the tool, and one Physics switch. Each replaces something we have now, so the app gets simpler as well as stronger.
Part 1

How Uniform got ideas Nomad doesn't have

One tool, every kind of data

The same Move edits a sculpt, a curve, a low-poly cage, UVs and lights. One paint stroke crosses a high-poly sculpt and a UV-mapped low-poly model. Pablo Dobarro said at the reveal that it has "no sculpting mode", only displacement brushes and deformers that work everywhere.

Switches that change every tool

Its overrides add a behaviour to all tools at once: snap to the sculpt (retopology), physics, pixel snapping, stencils. Retopology, cloth and pixel art come from those switches rather than separate features, because the base tools already work on the data.

Everything stays live

Primitives, SDF volumes and curves keep their handles until you apply them. Painted displacement shows on a low-poly model as you paint ("no baking, no high poly meshes"). Instances update across scenes.

One control for the whole look

The Surface View chart replaces the shading menu with one square. Of all its features, this is the one press coverage singled out as new.

What artists said

"daunting" with "no documentation", from a beta tester · "not made for artists", from someone who asked for a refund after a few hours · "basic functions hidden under numerous tabs" · and from the same thread, praise for low-poly modelling, texture painting, displacement on low-poly models and several views of one model, all things Nomad lacks.

Nomad forum, "Uniform 1.0 is now available" (May 2024); App Store listing; CG Channel's Uniform 1.0 review.

Lesson for us: composition is the engine, and showing it to people is the problem. We keep tools that work on everything, switches that change every tool, and live objects. We drop the module editor, the gallery search as the main way in, and names like M0/M1, "Delta Deformer" and "Surface Process".

Part 2

Do brush modulators pay off in real work?

What the evidence says

  • Painters mostly use pressure. Photoshop's usual setup is size and opacity on pen pressure; angle and roundness jitter are usually left at zero.
  • Variation lives in presets. In Procreate, colour jitter and dynamics are mostly set by brush makers, and one artist on r/ProCreate says the heavy hitters stick with simple brushes. That is one opinion, not a survey.
  • Full sensor editors hit a ceiling. Krita has drawing angle, distance, fade, speed and time sensors, each with a curve. Its forum asks for node graphs, and its bug tracker calls angle smoothing "a rework of our brush engine".
  • Sculptors buy repeated detail. One ZBrush stitch pack sells 100 curve brushes, 100 one-at-a-time brushes and 100 alphas, because spacing, alternation and placement are hard to set up by hand.
  • Uniform's own examples are toys: a dashed stroke and a rainbow, both on the paint brush.
Verdict

Worth building, but as an engine under named presets, not as a panel people have to wire. The value for a sculptor sits in three places:

  • Rhythm: stitches, chain links, rivets and scales, where every dab alternates, repeats or varies in a set pattern.
  • Variation: size, turn and depth jitter so stamped pores, rocks and bark don't look tiled. We already have Spray and Random turn.
  • Reading the surface (our own idea): strength or colour driven by cavity, curvature, facing or height, so wear, flush and grime land where they belong as you stroke.

One Variation slider on every brush, with presets for the rest, and the full modulator list kept under More for people who make brushes.

Part 3

Our own ideas, ranked

1

Live strokes: re-tune a stroke after you made it

nobody has thisL · design first
Idea

Pick any stroke on the History timeline, change its size, strength, brush or modulators, and the app replays it and every stroke after it. Fix the one Clay pass that was too strong an hour ago without redoing the rest.

Why we can

Our stroke replay is already bit-identical and seeded, and History already scrubs. Nomad 2.9 can only edit the last stroke; ZBrush and Blender can't edit one at all.

Cost and risk

Replay time grows with the strokes after the edit, so it needs checkpoints every N strokes and a background replay with a progress bar. It changes History, so it needs a design and an architect review before any code.

2

Brushes that read the sculpt

wowM
Idea

The modulator engine above, with surface inputs. Paint with "flush on ridges" and the colour reddens only on crests. Clay with "fill cavities" builds only in dents. Stamp pores that grow shallower toward flat areas.

Compared

ZBrush has cavity masking for a whole brush and Substance has smart masks for layers, but neither changes a dab as you stroke. Uniform's modulators read only the pen and the distance.

Replaces

Colour spray, Spray's scatter, Random turn and the two pressure curves become built-in modulators with the same behaviour and their old settings carried over. That's four controls folded into one system.

3

A look that follows the tool

wowS–M
Idea

The Look pad (try it below), plus a remembered spot per kind of tool. Pick Paint and the view slides toward colour; pick Clay and it slides back to shape; open Lights and it rises to lit. One drag sets each spot.

Compared

Uniform's chart is manual. Nomad, ZBrush and Blender switch shading by menu. None of them move the look for you.

Replaces

The shading list with MatCap, Flat, Material Preview and Rendered. They become the pad's four corners, so nothing is lost. The mask draws as an outline once colour is on.

shapeunlit lights onlyfull

Drag the dot, or pick a tool and watch the look move to that tool's spot. The masked cap goes dark in shape looks and becomes a dotted outline once colour is on.

4

One Physics switch for every move

wowM
Idea

A Physics switch that works on Grab, the gizmo, Pose, curves and Pin. The part you move drags the rest along as cloth, with gravity and a floor. On a curve, it becomes a rope that collides with itself, so you can tie knots, and the result is written back to live curve points.

Compared

This is Uniform's simulation switch with our solver. Blender needs a whole simulation setup. ZBrush Dynamics is a separate mode. Nomad has nothing like it.

Replaces

The Cloth brush's Drag and Gravity modes. The Cloth brush becomes a preset, Grab with Physics on, and Pinch and Expand stay as its options.

5

A row of switches that change every tool

M
Idea

Uniform's overrides, made visible: a row of chips in the context bar for Physics, Snap to sculpt, Stencil and Symmetry. Each lights up while it's on, and three saved sets swap between jobs such as retopo, cloth and print.

Fixes Uniform's flaw

In Uniform you load overrides from a gallery search and clear them by hand, so people forget they're on. Here they are always on screen.

Replaces

Per-tool copies of the same switch, such as the Model tools' own snapping and Front faces only, which is buried in Brush Options.

6

Live bake: sculpt detail shows on the game model as you work

game artistsL
Idea

Pair a quad model with the sculpt it came from. As you sculpt, a background job re-bakes only the texels of the parts you changed, and the low-poly model in the second view shows the new normal map within a moment.

Compared

Uniform paints displacement onto the low-poly model directly. Ours keeps the full sculpt and still skips the bake step. Substance and Marmoset bake on demand only.

Builds on

Quad Remesh, our bake and dirty-node tracking. The Surface plan's texel tiles make the partial re-bake possible.

7

Linked by default

perfM
Idea

Insert and Duplicate make linked copies, one mesh with many placings drawn with GPU instancing, and Make unique breaks the link. Colours work the same way: linked swatches, and Colourways A, B and C to flip a whole palette.

Compared

Uniform needs separate scenes for instances. Blender links with Alt+D, which people often don't know about. Nomad has instances, but not linked colours.

Replaces

Copies as the default for Insert. A thousand rivets cost the same to draw as one.

8

Live SDF shapes

biggest wowL · design first
Idea

Shapes that stay live inside one voxel object, in the order they're listed. Each joins, cuts, intersects or only paints, with a blend radius. Drag a handle and the voxels update.

Better than Uniform

Uniform ray marches every pixel and warns that it's slow when a shape fills the screen. We evaluate into our brick band, so the frame cost stays a mesh's.

Replaces

One-shot voxel booleans become one live list, and the boolean preview code merges into it.

Order

How I would build it

  • A. Modulator engine with presets and surface inputs, which replaces the four controls. It's medium-sized and doesn't touch the file format beyond adding fields at the end of a brush. Then the Look pad with each tool's spot.
  • B. Physics switch on Grab, the gizmo and Pin, then rope curves, then the tool switches row.
  • C. Live strokes, design and architect review first, because it touches History.
  • D. Linked copies, live bake, live SDF shapes, each with its own design.
