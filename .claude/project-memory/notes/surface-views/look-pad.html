One Look pad instead of a list of shading modes

Uniform's Surface View chart lets you slide from clay to colour to lit by dragging a dot in a square. We take that idea and fix what holds it back: our pad never goes black without lights, its spots have names and snap, it remembers a spot per tool, saved looks are pins on the same square instead of a second system, and the Render switch sits in its full corner. It replaces today's Shading menu list and the Solid options under it, so it adds no twin.

Try it

Drag the dot in the pad. Switch between Clay, Paint and Lights to see the look follow the tool. Hold 1 over the view (or press and hold the chip at the top right) and move the pointer to use the quick pad, then let go. The model is painted so the colour side shows something.

View light the pad's lower edge

Drag a dot to move a light that follows the camera; tap it to turn it off. The pad's top edge uses the scene's own lights (Look card, Lights), or the studio set when the scene has none.

What the two axes do

Across: shape to colour

Left is clay: no colour and extra cavity contrast, for sculpting. Colour comes in toward the middle while the form stays lit. Past the middle the shading fades, so on the right edge you see flat, unlit colour for painting.

Up: view light to scene light

The bottom edge is lit by the view light, which turns with the camera like a MatCap and never changes as you orbit. The top edge is lit by the scene's lights and environment. In between the two blend. Along the top, shading never fades, so the top right corner is the full look.

Today's modes are points on it

Solid with MatCap and Single colour is the bottom left. Solid with Studio light is the top left. Material Preview is the top right, and the Render switch is that corner's toggle. Solid with Flat light and Material colour is the bottom right. Nothing that exists is lost.

Cost to sculpting and orbit

  • The pad sends four numbers to the shader (colour, shape, scene light, contrast), 16 bytes added to the uniforms the mesh shader already reads. No new pass, buffer or texture.
  • On the edges and corners, where nearly everyone works, the shader runs only one of the two light paths, picked by a uniform branch, so those spots cost the same as today. Inside the pad it runs both, about 10 to 20 percent more fragment shading. I will measure that with gpubench at 10M before merging the shader PR.
  • Corners match today's modes pixel for pixel; ppm.py diff against the base build checks that in the first PR.
  • Dragging the dot only redraws the frame, the same as moving a slider in Look. Nothing touches the sculpt data, strokes or undo.

What goes away

  • The four Shading modes in the Shading menu (Wireframe, Solid, Material Preview, Rendered) and the Shading_Mode enum.
  • Solid's Lighting choice (Studio, MatCap, Flat) and the Single colour choice. Colour from keeps Material and Object.
  • The Wireframe shading mode, a twin of the Wireframe overlay.
  • Flat shading is renamed Facets, so it no longer reads like Flat colour.
  • Scene, settings and layout lines store the spot instead of the mode. As usual before release, old lines are passed over and not converted.

Build plan, four PRs

PR 1 · shader

Blend in the mesh shader

View_Shading becomes a spot plus Colour from and the view light. The shader takes the four weights; corners checked against today's modes, and gpubench numbers inside the pad. Settings and layout lines replaced.

PR 2 · interface

The pad

A ui.pad widget with snap spots and names. The Shading chip opens the Look card's Shading section, the same proc drawn in both places. The mode list goes, Wireframe stays an overlay, Render sits in the full corner. README and touch scenarios.

PR 3 · speed

Quick pad and follow the tool

Hold 1 or hold the chip with the pen: a small pad under the pointer with relative movement. Each tool kind remembers its spot. Saved in settings.

PR 4 · looks

Saved looks and the view light

Up to 4 pins on the pad, saved with the scene. The view light editor: 3 lights on a disc, base brightness, rim, contrast, or a MatCap in their place.

Your call, with my picks

  1. Replace the mode list with the pad. My pick is yes, since every mode we have is a point on it, and keeping both would be two UIs for one thing.
  2. Look follows the tool, on by default. My pick is on, with the switch beside the pad for people who want one fixed look.
  3. Saved looks as pins, at most 4. My pick is yes. Uniform's Static views become pins, so there is one system instead of two.

If you agree with all three, I build the four PRs in order, each checked and merged before the next.
