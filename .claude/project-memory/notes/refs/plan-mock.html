Reference images: one board, three places

A plan for reference images that takes PureRef's board, ZBrush's Spotlight and image planes, Nomad's overlay and Blender's view-only planes, and fixes what each gets wrong. Nothing is built yet: the picks at the bottom decide the build. The mock below is live; it is a sketch of the iPad screen, so everything is approximate except the behaviour.

What the mock shows

One board in one card

  • Refs is a card like Look or Layers: it floats, pins or sits in a column, and a rail button opens it. Inside is a PureRef board: pan with one finger on empty space, pinch or wheel to zoom, drag images, tap to select.
  • Add opens Photos or Files, Paste takes the clipboard, dropping files works anywhere, and Snap view puts a picture of the viewport on the board, for before and after.
  • Arrange packs everything without gaps, Same height normalizes, Note adds text. Flip, Grey, Crop and Rotate sit on the selection's row; none of them changes the file you added.

The same image in three places

  • On the board, always. That is its home.
  • On view: pinned over the viewport at an opacity, like ZBrush's Spotlight in Reference mode and Nomad's overlay. It never takes a touch while you sculpt; it fades under the brush so it never hides what you sculpt.
  • In scene: a plane facing Front, Side or Top, shown only when the camera looks down its axis (Blender), behind or through the model.
  • While the Refs card is open, overlays show a tab to move them and a corner to size them. Close the card and they are just pictures: no edit mode to remember.

Not taken, and why

  • PureRef's window over other apps (always on top, click-through): an OS window trick from the desktop. iPadOS cannot do it, and our refs already sit over our own view, where they are needed.
  • Images from a web address: needs the network permission and a privacy note for the stores (sm-store) for something paste already covers.
  • Drawing on the board (PureRef 2.1 lines): parked until someone asks; notes cover labelling.
  • Spotlight Projection (painting or sculpting the image onto the model): a real need, but it belongs to Paint layers and alphas (stencil painting), not to references. A later plan can use a ref as the stencil's image.
  • Image to mesh (Spotlight's mesh from images): a modelling tool, not a reference; out of scope.
  • Tiling and hue tweaks from the Spotlight dial: tools for painting with an image, not for looking at one.

Budget: no hitch on a stroke

Never on the stroke path

  • Decoding runs on a worker, as environment panoramas do; a picture shows a grey box with a spinner until it is ready, never stalling a frame.
  • Overlays are drawn with the interface, after the viewport, so the idle cache and partial redraw of the mesh are untouched: 0 ms added to sculpt passes. Planes add one textured quad each to the viewport pass (under 0.05 ms for ten on the 3060 Ti, to measure on the Mali).
  • Fade under the brush is one alpha per overlay per frame on the CPU.
  • Timing to prove it: stroke press, release and undo with 20 refs shown, against none.

Memory

  • The GPU copy is at most 2048 px on its long edge on tablets and 4096 on the desktop, with mips; the original stays in the file. Twenty 12-megapixel photos cost about 420 MB at full size, about 110 MB this way.
  • A Refs memory limit (256 MB on tablets by default, in Settings next to Mesh size limits) drops the GPU copies of refs not seen lately to 256 px thumbnails.
  • Exit stats: refs=, refs_gpu_mb=, refs_decode_ms=.

The build in six PRs

Each PR ships something usable and deletes nothing that is still needed. R1 to R3 give the PureRef board and overlays; R4 the planes; R5 and R6 the finish.

R1
Refs store, file and board.

The scene's refs (image bytes, board place), a REFS chunk in the .sculpt (scene version bump, no old reader), decoding on a worker, GPU copies with mips through ui.image; the Refs card with pan, zoom, move, scale, select and delete; Add, Paste and drop on the desktop; exit stats.

~700 lines
R2
Tidy and edit.

Arrange (packed), Same height, Fit all, multi-select, Flip, Grey, Rotate, Crop (non-destructive), notes, lock, Snap view; Refs steps in History.

~600 lines
R3
On view.

Drag a ref onto the view to pin it; opacity; tab and corner handles while the card is open; Show refs dock button; fade under the brush; Pick colour into the Colours card.

~500 lines
R4
In scene.

Planes for Front, Back, Side, Top or free; only in its view; Behind or Through; one new textured-quad shader with its committed .spv, .msl and .wgsl; the planes listed in the Scene card with show and lock. Needs a design note first (new shader).

~650 lines
R5

Brow and Chin marks on a plane that meet the Proportion guides; Copy board from another scene; the Refs memory limit in Settings.

Your picks before the build

1. Where the board lives

Recommended: a Refs card (floats, pins, docks, as in the mock). The other choice is a fifth space tab, Refs, with the board full screen; it gives more room but hides the model while you tidy.

2. Refs per scene or for the app

Recommended: per scene, inside the .sculpt, with Copy board from another scene. The other choice is one app-wide library of refs shared by every scene, which Send to tablet would not carry.

3. Original size kept

Recommended: keep the original bytes up to 8192 px and shrink anything larger once on adding. The other choice keeps everything as is, which can make scene files hundreds of megabytes bigger.

4. Projection paint

Recommended: parked for a Paint layers plan. The other choice adds it as R7 now.
