Taken from

  • ZBrush UV Master: one tap unwraps, and a guide brush paints where seams may and may not go.
  • Blender and 3D-Coat: live unwrap that re-flattens as seams change, pins, and stretch shown on the model.
  • RizomUV: stretch colours that mean the same everywhere, and a packer that fills the square.

Avoided

  • Blender's edge-select mode and keys just to mark a seam.
  • ZBrush's unwrap with no editor at all: you cannot move or straighten one island.
  • A separate UV app and a round trip through files.

Done better

  • Seams drawn with the pen on the sculpt, snapping to crevices, re-flattened in milliseconds per stroke.
  • Peel: the model unfolds into its layout, so anyone sees what a UV is.
  • Seams go first where nobody looks: cavities, the underside, behind things (the occlusion the app already computes).

Texture paint here too

Taken from

  • Substance Painter and Marmoset: a layer stack where each layer carries colour, roughness and metal, and masks made from the shape.
  • Procreate and Nomad: painting feels like drawing: the brush, its pressure and the colour well stay where the hand is.
  • ZBrush Spotlight: a picture laid over the view and painted through.

Avoided

  • Substance's rule that maps be baked before a smart mask works.
  • Blender's texture paint: one image, no layers, slow brushes.
  • Polypaint's limit: the paint is only as sharp as the mesh is dense.

Done better

  • Masks read the sculpt itself, live: cavities, height, facing up, thickness, even the detail between two subdivision levels.
  • One brush stroke paints the three channels at once, in 3D across seams and with symmetry.
  • Paint now, UVs later: the same layers can live on vertices of a 20M sculpt, then move onto texels when the object gets UVs.

Weathered bronze material stack

The stack is the same graph shown as layers: each row is a Mix node and its mask. Open Graph to see every wire.

Taken from

  • three.js node editor: the graph floats over the scene and you see the model behind it.
  • Blender and Unreal: preview any node on the model with one tap (Node Wrangler's preview).
  • Substance Designer: every node is a picture, so each node shows its own thumbnail.

Avoided

  • Blender graphs that do not survive export: only its Principled node reaches glTF.
  • Node editors made for a mouse: small sockets, right-click menus, keys to add nodes.
  • Compiling a new GPU shader on every change, which stalls and differs per driver.

Done better

  • Every graph exports: it runs into colour, roughness, metal and normal maps, so glTF, USD, Blender and the path tracer all see exactly this.
  • Stack and Graph are one thing: touch users work in layers, and the wires are there when wanted.
  • Probe: press on the model and each node shows its value at that point.

How the others do it, and what we do

From what the app has today: Quad Remesh lays planar UV charts on quad objects, Bake maps writes normal, displacement, base colour, ORM and ID maps and a glb, an object shows its base colour, ORM and normal maps in the viewport and in Full render (up to 64 textured objects under Vulkan with texture arrays), polypaint paints per vertex with Sculpt, Both and Paint channels, and each polygroup can carry its own material. What is missing is everything in between: editing UVs, painting into textures, and building a material.

Wow features

W1 · Peel

A slider unfolds the model into its UV layout and folds it back. Seams, stretch and islands become obvious to anyone, and it doubles as a check before baking.

W2 · Seams that hide themselves

Auto seams weigh how visible each edge is from around the model (the occlusion the app already traces), cavities and the underside, then polygroups and creases, so the first unwrap is already good.

W3 · Masks from the sculpt

Smart masks read the high mesh itself, live: cavity, height, facing up, thickness, polygroups, the sculpt mask, and the detail between two subdivision levels (exactly what you sculpted on top of the form). No bake, no waiting.

W4 · Paint now, UVs later

Layers live on vertices of a 20M sculpt, which needs no UVs. When the object gets UVs (Quad Remesh, Unwrap) every layer moves onto texels in one step, through the bake the app already has.

W5 · Probe

Press on the model and every node shows its value there, as numbers and swatches. Finding why one spot looks wrong takes one touch, not a chain of previews.

W6 · Every graph exports

The graph's result is maps, so what you see is what glTF, USD, the Blender link, the path tracer and a game engine get. No other node editor promises that.

W7 · The graph over the model

Overlay with a backdrop slider, Split as a Lens pane, Full with the model in the mini view, and Hold to peek. The model is never more than a touch away.

W8 · Material time-lapse

Graph edits and paint strokes are History steps, so the existing timeline and time-lapse replay a material being made as they replay a sculpt.

Build order

S1

UV core. UVs on any base mesh (triangles too), seams as an edge set saved per object, auto seams, the flattener and packer, stretch view, Peel. A UV pane that only shows. Benchmarked against xatlas.

S2

UV editing. Seam pen and eraser on the model with live re-flattening, the guide brush, the UV pane's island select, move, pinch and twist, Pack, Straighten, pins, texel density, mirrored halves.

S3

Paint layers on vertices. Roughness, metal and height beside polypaint's colour, a layer stack with blend modes and opacity, fill layers, Surface space and its shelf pages.

S4

Texture paint. Texel layers in sparse 128-texel tiles, GPU dabs in 3D across seams, tile undo, gutters, painting in the UV pane, stencils, the move of vertex layers onto texels.

S5

Masks from the sculpt. Cavity, height, facing, thickness, level difference, polygroups and the sculpt mask as live generators, and material looks built from them.

S6

Shader graph. The graph and its kernels into the maps, Stack view first, then Graph with Overlay, Split and Full, node previews and Probe.

S7

Out. Bake the result to files, glTF and USD with the maps, texture sets per material, UDIM tiles, the Blender link carrying maps.

Choices for Bolt

Where it lives. Default: a fourth space, Surface, with UV, Paint and Shade pages. The other way: UV under Model and Paint and Shade under Render.

How the graph runs. Default: into maps through fixed kernels (exports, no compiling). The other way: graphs compiled into live shaders, which allows view-dependent nodes but breaks export and needs Slang on the device.

The flattener. Default: our own in Odin, measured against xatlas (MIT) on the same fixtures, keeping whichever wins, as with Quad Remesh.
