Docking: tabs, windows and drag to rearrange

The split view becomes a docking system. Every pane is a frame of tabs (as in imgui), every frame behaves like a window (pop out, pin, minimise, maximise, snap, as on Windows and Linux), and any tab or frame can be dragged to a new place while the others slide out of its way (Blender's rearranging, animated). The egg, UVs, paint and graph all still run.

Where I push back

Pop out means a floating frame inside the app, not an OS window, except on desktops.

iPadOS can open a second scene but each costs memory and a GPU surface, and Android gives one window. On Windows, Linux and the Mac a frame can still become its own OS window, as the view windows already do; elsewhere it floats inside the app.

Animate the picture, resize once.

A 3D frame that changes size each animation frame would make the GPU reallocate its target 17 times in 280 ms and redraw a big mesh each time. The app should slide a scaled snapshot of the old picture and draw the real one once at the end, so a reflow costs one frame of rendering. Interface kinds (UVs, graph, stack) can animate for real.

A preview while dragging, not a live reflow.

Moving every frame while a tab is still in the air means constant resizes and a layout that jumps under the finger. The tinted rectangle shows exactly where it lands; the reflow animates once, on letting go.

Few states, named plainly.

A frame is Docked, Floating, Minimised or Maximised; Pin is a switch a floating frame has (on top, kept across layouts and spaces, never fading under a stroke). Anything more (shading a window to its bar, always-on-bottom, nested floating groups) adds states people have to learn for little gain.

Tabs hide work, so keep them few.

Tabs suit kinds you switch between (Graph, Stack, UVs). Two 3D views are better side by side than tabbed. The bar doubles as the frame's head, so a tab costs no extra height.

Limits.

Six docked frames and three floating at most, eight tabs a frame. A tablet has room for about four docked frames before each is too small to sculpt in.

When a frame with several tabs fills the screen

There are two ways for a frame to get big, and they want different answers. Maximise works the way the Lens viewport already does: the frame fills the whole window, edge to edge, and the top bar, rail and shelf float over it, so you keep working with the tools. Its tab bar becomes one more small island under the top bar. Full screen hides everything for looking, presenting or a long graph session. Try both in screen 1: pick Shade, tabs, then press the frame's maximise or full-screen button.

My pick for MaximiseA · Tabs stay on the frame's bar

One rule everywhere: a frame's tabs are on its own bar, maximised or not. When maximised, the bar floats as a small island at the top left, under the top bar, and the frame runs edge to edge beneath it. Nothing jumps, and the window buttons stay where your hand learned them.

The alternativeB · Tabs move into the top bar

The bar disappears and the tabs ride in the top bar's middle island with Restore beside them. It saves one island and looks tidy, but tabs now live in two places depending on state, and the top bar gets crowded on an iPad in portrait. Fine as a setting, wrong as the default.

My pick for Full screenC · A tab pill that fades away

Full screen keeps only a small pill at the top: leave, the tabs, and Overview. It fades after two seconds and comes back when the pointer or a finger reaches the top. Swipe the pill sideways to go to the next tab (the content slides with it), tap a tab, or press Ctrl+Tab. On a phone the pill shows dots instead of names. Esc or the button leaves.

My better ideaD · Overview

Every frame and tab at once, as a map of live cards laid out like the real layout (Mission Control, the iPad's app switcher). Tap a card to go there, whatever state you were in. Drag a card onto another frame to move that tab, drag a frame's head onto another to swap them. It is the touch-first way to rearrange: big targets, the whole layout in view, no aiming at a small arrow while a frame is in the air. The five-way drop targets stay for the mouse and for precise splits.

Does the top bar need a redesign?

Honestly, no: it needs one new piece, not a new shape. The top bar is app-level (file, undo, the space, the object, search, history, settings); frames own their own business. If the top bar started carrying frame controls it would change content every time focus moved, and on an iPad in portrait there is no room. So:

Add a layout chip to the middle island.

It names the layout and how many frames it has (Three columns · 3 frames) and opens Overview. While a frame is maximised it also carries Restore, so getting back never means hunting for a button on the frame. On an iPad or a phone it shrinks to an icon and a count.

Keep the three islands as they are.

The left island (file, undo), the middle (spaces, object, now layout) and the right (search, history, settings) were rebuilt in October and still fit. The layout chip replaces the rail's layout button, so the rail loses one button rather than the top bar gaining a row.

Full screen hides the top bar entirely.

Tools are still reachable through the pen and finger gestures and the keyboard; one swipe down from the top edge (or the pill's button) brings the app back.

The honest part

Most people will never drag a frame onto an arrow.

On a tablet, presets plus Overview cover nearly all rearranging. The drop targets are worth having, but they are the desktop's tool. I would build Overview in D2 next to drag and drop, and make it the first thing the layout chip opens, so the touch path is the easy one.

Tabs inside a frame are for kinds, not for 3D views.

Full screen with tabs works best when the tabs are different things (Graph, Stack, UVs): a swipe flips between them. Two 3D views stay side by side, as before.

One gesture for full screen, nothing hidden.

Full screen is the frame's button, Shift+F or F11 on a keyboard, and a pinch out with four fingers on a tablet, and it always shows the pill for two seconds on entry so nobody gets stuck.

Plan

This replaces V1 to V4 of the split-view plan. The Surface stages (UV, Paint, Shade) then arrive as kinds of tab.

D1

Frames and tabs. The grid's tree holds frames of any number of children per row or column (today's binary splits read in as such); a frame holds tabs; a tab is a kind with its own state (today's view pane becomes the 3D kind). The tab bar replaces the pane's chip head. Workspaces save tabs and frames on new lines older apps skip. Ships with 3D tabs only.

D2

Drag and drop. Tabs and frames dragged by their bar; the five-way target over a frame, edge targets, Swap, the landing preview; dropping elsewhere pops out. Every drop is a layout undo step. The reflow animation with the snapshot rule.

D3

Windows. Pop out and dock back where it came from; Pin; See-through and hold to peek; Minimise to the tray at the off-hand corner; Maximise and restore; Snap to a half; on desktops, Open in its own window through the view-window code.

D4

The shelf follows the frame, portrait and phone. The focused frame's active tab picks the shelf page; side by side stacks in portrait; on a phone the first frame fills and the others are chips in a sheet.

D5

First new kinds. Texture and Reference picture; then UVs, Stack and Graph with the Surface stages.

Choices for Bolt

Pop out on tablets. Recommended: a floating frame inside the app. The other way: real iPad windows, which need a second scene and more memory.

Reflow animation for 3D frames. Recommended: snapshot slide, one real draw at the end. The other way: true resize every frame, smoother on small meshes and slow on big ones.

Tabs of 3D views. Recommended: allowed, with a hint that side by side shows both. The other way: 3D views never tab.
