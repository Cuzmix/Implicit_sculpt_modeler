Docks and Cards

v5.6

Anything anywhere: any button on any dock, docks on any edge or floating, edited in place. Windows show cards as tabs and snap to a side. Each space keeps its own layout.

The idea in one line

The rail, the shelf, the tools strip and the bars were one thing with different restrictions. v5 drops the restrictions: there is only a dock, it takes any button, and it goes on any edge or floats. Freedom is in placement; the guardrails are in a few things that can't break.

What changed from v4

  • Any button on any dock. Tools, cards, groups, commands and spaces all mix. The rail and the shelf are just two docks in the starting layout.
  • Docks go anywhere. Drag a dock by its handle: near an edge it snaps there (anywhere along it), elsewhere it floats. + New dock makes another.
  • Edit in place. Hold any dock (or tap Edit) and every dock wiggles: drag buttons between docks, off to remove, + adds from a searchable picker, ⋯ sets rows or columns and names. No modal sheet to drag out of.
  • Stacks. Drop a window, tab or card button on the square above or below a floating window and they stack, one under another, as in ImGui. A stack moves as one by the grip on top, scrolls when it is taller than the screen, and a window pulled out by its bar floats on its own again. Long docks scroll too.
  • Windows: tabs, stacks and snap. Sideways splits are gone; a drop joins as a tab, stacks above or below, snaps to a side, or floats. Snapped windows stack top to bottom beside the dock; the view stays where it is.
  • Soft alignment. A floating window or dock you drag lines up with its neighbours within about 10pt: same left, right, top or bottom, centred, or just beside or under with a small gap. A faint line, fading at its ends, shows the match and fades out when you move on or let go.
  • Pin is explicit. Moving no longer pins on its own. Snapping does, because a snapped window is placed on purpose.
  • Minimise folds, as today. Any window, floating or snapped, folds to its bar and stays where it is; tap the bar's arrow or its dock button to unfold.
  • A group is a saved window. A window's ⋯ has Add to dock: one tab adds that card, several add a group. There are no separate group settings.
  • Each space has its own layout. Sculpt, Model and Render each keep their docks and windows; Render starts with the Look cards snapped.

New problems freedom brings, and the guardrail for each

Lost the way backMenu and Search are locked: they can move between docks but never be removed, and Settings always has Reset this space.
ClutterDocks snap to edges and to a grid when they float; a new dock starts empty and small; the starting layouts are good enough that most people never edit.
Covering the modelFloating windows are temporary unless pinned; a folded window is just its bar. Docks may float anywhere, middle included, because they are small and you put them there.
Accidental editsNothing moves outside edit mode except windows. Edit mode starts only with a hold on a dock or the Edit command, and Done or a tap on the view ends it.
Same card in many placesButtons are shortcuts, so a card may have several. The card itself is only ever in one window: opening it elsewhere moves it.
Different screensA layout stores edge positions as fractions, not pixels, so it survives a rotation or a bigger screen; phone-sized screens get their own starting layout.
Left-handed usersA Mirror layout command flips every dock and snapped window left to right.
Support and testsEvery button and window has a name; touch scenarios already tap by name, and Reset gives a known layout.

How I'd balance it

  1. Free to place, hard to break. Placement is free; removing the way back is impossible.
  2. Snap, not pixels. Edges, fractions along them, a grid for floating docks. Layouts stay tidy without effort.
  3. Viewport first. Anything that would cover the model is temporary unless you say otherwise.
  4. One verb per job. Tap opens, hold edits, drag moves, pin keeps. No second way to do the same thing.
  5. Good defaults per space. Freedom is for the people who want it; everybody else gets a layout that already works.

Decided with you

  • Floating docks exist, and a dock can float anywhere, the middle of the screen included.
  • Minimise is today's fold: the window folds to its bar, floating or snapped.

Things to try

  1. Tap a card button: its window pops out beside the dock. Tap the view and it goes away.
  2. Drag a window's bar or a tab: guides appear. The middle square joins a window as a tab; the side squares snap to that side. Drop elsewhere and it floats.
  3. Snap a window to a side: it fills that side beside the dock. Snap a second one: they stack.
  4. Drag a card button or a window onto the square below another floating window: they stack. Add a few more and the stack scrolls; drag it by its grip; pull one out by its bar.
  5. Pin two windows (or float two docks in edit mode), then drag one under or beside the other: near a match it lines up, with a faint line showing which edge.
  6. Minimise any window: it folds to its bar where it is. Tap the arrow, or its dock button, to unfold.
  7. A window's ⋯ → Add to dock turns it into a button (a group if it has several tabs).
  8. Hold any dock button (or tap Edit on the top dock): every dock wiggles. Drag buttons between docks or off to remove; drag a dock by its handle to another edge or into the open; + adds; ⋯ sets rows, names, delete.
  9. Switch to Render: a different layout. Back to Sculpt: yours is as you left it.
