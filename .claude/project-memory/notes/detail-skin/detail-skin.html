Detail Skin

A cheaper way to sculpt fine detail. Past the size your mesh can hold, strokes land in a skin of texels on the surface instead of needing more polygons. When you're ready, the skin becomes real polygons, or exports as maps for a game. Bolt's ask: "push more detail while sculpting, and when they are ready they can bake back, like a new way of sculpting that's cheaper".

How it works

Six ideas that make up the skin

1

A skin you never have to unwrap

coreL · design first
What

Each chunk of the mesh (our BVH nodes, about 1,000 triangles) gets its own patch of texels at the same density in world units, packed automatically. You never see UVs.

Brushes

Draw, Clay, Crease, Smooth, Flatten, Layer and alpha stamps write height into the skin. Mask and symmetry apply as usual, and strokes cross patch edges because neighbouring patches share their border texels.

Keeps our rules

Stamps run on the CPU job pool over 64×64 tiles, so replays stay bit-identical. Only touched tiles go to the GPU, and undo is per tile, as voxel bricks already work.

2

Each stroke splits by size

wowM
What

The broad part of a stroke moves mesh vertices and the fine part goes into the skin, with no mode to switch. The split sits at the mesh's own edge length, and a slider moves it.

Why

A skin can't change the silhouette, and a mesh can't hold pores cheaply. Splitting every stroke gives you both, and you don't have to think about it.

Not in the skin

Grab, Move, Snake hook, Pose and Cloth always move the mesh. The skin rides along on the surface as it moves.

3

Apply and Squeeze: back and forth

wowM
Apply

Turns the skin into real polygons when you're ready: a new subdivision level, or HD detail, displaced by the skin. One undo step.

Squeeze

Goes the other way. Take a heavy 20M sculpt, move its fine detail into the skin and decimate the mesh, so sculpting stays fast. Apply brings it back.

Compared

ZBrush HD Geometry shows detail only inside one small window and only renders it. Nanite and micro-meshes handle drawing, not sculpting. I found nothing that moves detail both ways.

4

Maps for games, with no bake

M
What

A game model (Quad Remesh with UVs) reads its normal, height and displacement maps straight from the sculpt's mesh and skin, re-sampled live for the tiles a stroke changed.

Better than baking

Skew, waviness, cage seams and ray misses come from projecting a far-away high poly. Our game model sits on the sculpt it came from, and the problem map tints any spot where it doesn't.

Exports

Presets for Unreal (green down), Unity, Godot and Blender (green up), and MikkTSpace tangents ported bit for bit (zlib licence), since ours match "not to the bit" today.

5

Real edges near the camera

M–L
What

Far away, the skin is a normal in the shader. Close up, a compute pass splits the nearest triangles and pushes them out by the skin, so profiles and silhouettes show the detail too.

Why last

Normal shading already sells most detail while you sculpt. This is for close-ups and renders, and it costs GPU time.

Builds on

Our compute culling and indirect draws, and the studio and path-traced render, which can trace the displaced surface.

6

Extras the skin makes easy

S each
Seamless tiles

A flat skin whose edges wrap, so brick, bark or scales tile with no seam (the mock does this). Tiles then become alphas and stencils.

Game distance

A slider shows the mip level a game uses far away (in the mock), so you know whether pores survive before you export.

Maps in

Load a normal or height map, or a photo, into the skin and sculpt on it. A normal map turns into height through a Poisson solve, so it doesn't drift.

Risks

What could go wrong

  • Patch borders. Strokes must cross from one patch to the next with no step. Border texels are shared and kept in sync, and stage 1 includes a test that strokes across every border of a sphere and checks the result.
  • The mesh changing under the skin. Remesh, dynamic topology and Decimate change the patches, so the skin is re-sampled onto the new ones by ray, as a swap undo entry. Dynamic topology inside a stroke stays off while a skin exists, at least in the first version.
  • Height only. The skin can't hold overhangs. That's fine for pores, wrinkles, scales and engraving. Sideways offsets (vector displacement, 6 bytes a texel) could come later if needed.
  • Process rules. It brings new object data, a file chunk and GPU uploads, so it needs a written design and an architect review before any code.

How this fits the plans already open

The Surface plan's texture painting needs per-texel tiles too. If the skin's tile store also holds colour, roughness and metal, one stroke can paint and sculpt at once, and paint works on any sculpt without UVs. I'd design K1 as that shared store, which makes the Surface plan's painting stages cheaper later.
