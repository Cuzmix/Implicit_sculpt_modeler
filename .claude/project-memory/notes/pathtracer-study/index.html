What makes Josef Pelz's Pathtracer fast, and what we can take

A study of Pathtracer 3.0.1 for TouchDesigner (MIT, by Josef Pelz), read from all 18 pages of its documentation, set against our own render mode in src/render as of odin-sculpt at #439. Research only; no code changed. 31 October 2026.

The short answer

It is fast mostly for three reasons, and special for two more. None of them is a secret trick: it is a careful, modern real-time path tracer that leans hard on the GPU vendor's hardware and denoiser.

  1. It traces far fewer pixels than it shows. The default denoiser is NVIDIA DLSS Ray Reconstruction at Balanced quality, which traces a little under 60% of the width and height (about a third of the pixels), one path per pixel per frame, and lets a neural network denoise and upscale to full size. This is the single largest speed factor, and it is the one we cannot copy: it is neural, NVIDIA-only and under NVIDIA's own licence.
  2. Rays run on ray-tracing hardware. TouchDesigner 2025 builds a driver acceleration structure for a POP and exposes Vulkan ray queries to GLSL, and the Pathtracer uses it. On a Mac, where TouchDesigner has no hardware ray tracing through MoltenVK, it walks its own tree in software. We already do both, and on Apple we go further with Metal ray tracing.
  3. The kernel only contains what the scene uses. "Optimize" compiles a trace specialised to the current settings (no environment code without an environment, no haze, sheen, thin film, dispersion or see-through tests unless something uses them). Until you press it, a flexible trace that handles every setting stands in, so changing a setting never pauses. We have one kernel with every branch in it. This is the best idea here for us, and it is cheap.
  4. Its light sampling is tuned so noise stays low at 1 sample. Lights of every kind are normalised to give the same light at the same Strength, the environment is always importance sampled, a glowing surface can be told not to light the scene, and a firefly clamp limits only indirect light. "Glass Shadows" traces caustics through smooth glass directly.
  5. It does a lot that sculpt renderers usually skip: a full layered material (clear coat, sheen, thin film, transmission with absorption and dispersion, opacity and cutout), seven light kinds including any mesh made to glow, and haze with beams, drifting noise, 3D-texture density and multiple scattering.

What changed from 2.1

  • A rebuild, not an update: 2.1 scenes are rebuilt with the new components.
  • Denoising narrowed to two methods: SVGF and OptiX removed; DLSS for motion, Accumulate for stills. Plain averaging renamed Accumulate.
  • Optimize: specialised traces, so changing a setting no longer pauses TouchDesigner.
  • Lights gained direction and size (2.1 had rectangle, circle, point shining both ways); a mesh light replaced "a shape wired into the light"; Strength is always the total output.
  • Haze, points and lines, glTF and FBX straight from their COMPs, TouchDesigner's own geometry and lights as a Render TOP sees them.
  • Materials: "Transparency" became Transmission with Absorption Colour and Distance; attributes (Color, Roughness, Metallic, Transmission, Emit) always read; one material per PT GEO.
  • macOS support (software trace, Accumulate only), sRGB and dither, Exposure, Max Samples, eight views.
  • Environment importance sampling no longer a switch.

What we should adopt, in order

1
Specialised render kernels, chosen automatically

Turn render mode's feature branches (image environment, maps, depth of field, paint, textured items, ray query) into Vulkan specialization constants and Metal function constants on the same compiled shader. When the scene's features change, compile the matching variant on a worker thread with the pipeline cache, and keep tracing with the general kernel until it is ready, as his flexible trace does. No button: we know what the scene uses. Measure ms per sample before and after on the 3060 Ti and the iPad; expect most gain where registers limit occupancy.

Effort: 2 PRs. Touches shaders, so a design first (AGENTS.md section 2). Fits under Metal and Vulkan alike.

2
Render mode that stays clean while moving, without a neural network

His answer is DLSS. Ours, already planned as stage 4 of the render plan: trace at reduced size while the camera moves (we have half resolution), reproject the history as Fast mode does, ReSTIR for direct light, and a temporal-plus-spatial denoiser split by signal (diffuse and specular apart) with motion, roughness and hit-distance guides. His dropping SVGF says plain SVGF smears; separating signals and using hit distance for specular is what the open designs that followed SVGF do.

Effort: the largest item, several PRs. Biggest effect on how the app feels.

3
Area lights with normalised strength

Rectangle and disk lights, a radius for point and spot, an angle for the sun, and his rule that every kind at the same Strength lights a nearby surface equally, so switching kind keeps the brightness. Soft shadows on a sculpt are what studio renders are judged by.

Effort: 1 to 2 PRs. The lights belong to the lights work; the scene file takes new fields at the end of its chunk.

4
Clear coat and sheen, then transmission

His layer set is a good checklist for our stage 6. Add thin film there cheaply, keep subsurface (which he lacks) for skin, and leave each layer out of the kernel when no material uses it, which item 1 makes free.

5
Small things

Clamp only indirect light, and let the user set the clamp. Show albedo, normal, depth and material id as passes. Draw "No light" on the render instead of falling back silently. Stable Planes style guides once mirrors or glass exist.

–
Not for us

DLSS (neural, NVIDIA-only, proprietary), haze and glass caustics (showpieces, not sculpting needs), spheres and tubes for points and lines, Lock Input.

What the licence allows

The Pathtracer, PT GEO, PT MAT and PT LIGHT components and the DLSS plugin's own code are MIT (Copyright 2026 Josef Pelz). MIT lets us use, copy, change and ship any of it, in a paid app and on the App Store and Google Play, as long as the copyright and permission notice go with the copies. It gives no warranty.

Not covered by MIT: NVIDIA's DLSS runtime inside DlssTOP.dll (NVIDIA's licence, and out by Bolt's rule anyway), and TDFam, which turns the components into an operator family (dotsimulate and Function Store's own licence).

In practice we would port ideas, not files: his code is GLSL for TouchDesigner's GLSL POP, ours is Slang under our own backends. Ideas need no licence. If we ever lift a shader function close to verbatim, we add his MIT notice beside it and to the app's licences screen.
